# Globe instrument

Implemented slice of [#23](https://github.com/mESmaC/oia/issues/23) in the desktop workbench ([desktop-workbench.md](desktop-workbench.md)): an interactive CesiumJS globe inside the Tauri WebView, drawing a bundled synthetic case in the [visual language v2](../design/visual-language-v2.md) instrument language over live basemap tiles from a registered online source. It follows the owner's direction of 2026-11-03 that online sources, satellite imagery included, are part of the product and preferred free ([ADR 0008 §10](../adr/0008-d01-workspace-renderer.md#10-amendment-registered-online-imagery-in-the-webview-2026-11-03)).

Requirements touched: `GEO-01` (areas, points, approximate locations, reported local time with uncertainty), `GEO-02` (imagery with capture statement, coverage, source status, and finer real imagery as the camera closes in), `UX-01` to `UX-05`, and the globe and camera subsets of `A02` and `A04`. Persistent cases, clustering on the globe, footprints and viewing cones, tracks, terrain, and the `#24` to `#26` context sources are not in this slice.

## Run

### Highway camera imagery (#164)

The **Cameras** inspector tab adds real Caltrans highway ground views beside the satellite/aerial resolution ladder (`GEO-02`, `GEO-04`, `CAM-01`/`CAM-02`; A02/A04/A09 subsets). Choose one California district (1–12), press **Load district cameras**, select a camera from the keyboard-accessible selector or cyan globe points, then **Request current still**. Selection flies to a 3 km display view; **Fly to camera** repeats it. Camera locations are provider assertions. The ellipsoid anchor is a display convention, not a camera-height observation. Only the selected traffic camera receives a callout, keeping the district's other markers readable. The Layers panel toggles this real catalog independently of the synthetic ALPR equipment fixture; its Fly to action frames the loaded camera extent.

The still is a separate ground-view card, never stretched over terrain or merged with overhead imagery. No calibrated azimuth, pitch, field of view, range, height or footprint is supplied. The provider's road-direction text stays text. The catalog edit date/time is preserved as supplied without assigning a timezone; it is **not** image capture time. Capture time, latency and freshness remain unknown even when the HTTP request succeeds or bytes change. Repeat retrieval of identical bytes is identified without manufacturing a capture time or scene-change conclusion. Every preview remains ineligible as event evidence. A catalog in-service flag and an HTTP-200 JPEG do not establish a working scene feed: the provider can serve an unavailable notice. Original content is displayed unchanged, without automatic scene/placeholder classification. Neither current cameras nor the satellite/aerial mosaics establish the historical case scene.

`crates/oia-app/src/globe/traffic.rs` owns a provider-specific Rust HTTP adapter, normalization, source state and budgets. This is an explicit session-only browsing slice, not completion of the shared registry/jobs/store contracts (#13/#14/#11), the #28 retained-frame ingestion kernel, D06/D07 provider selection for the full product, or full A04/A09 acceptance. Required follow-up after #166 merges: move this provisional fetch to its shared `oia-ingest` transport/registry, preserving these budgets, destination restrictions and source semantics; no unmerged implementation is copied here. It does not register previews as retained evidentiary frames. The HTTP dependency is pinned `reqwest 0.13.5` (blocking worker, rustls/platform certificate verification); `image 0.25.10` reads JPEG dimensions. No production subprocess or hosted workflow is introduced.

Registered acquisition is limited to `https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/data/d{district}/cctv/cctvStatusD{district:02}.json` and the exact current JPEG URLs found beneath that same district's `/cctv/image/<directory>/<file>.jpg`. All image URLs reject other origins, credentials, encoded/traversal/query paths and previous-frame directories. Redirects and proxies are disabled; TLS verification remains enabled. A custom resolver permits only the fixed official hostname and supplies checked public destination addresses directly to the connector, rejecting private, loopback, link-local, multicast, protocol/transition and documentation ranges. DNS resolution remains inside the HTTP connection/request deadlines. Tokio 1.54.1 supplies the asynchronous lookup in reqwest's worker runtime. There are no streaming-video, archive, alternate-host or synthetic fallback requests. Only this public highway catalog is supported; home/interior sources and arbitrary camera URLs cannot be submitted.

- One HTTP worker at a time across catalog/still requests; 20-second whole-request and 8-second connection deadlines. Cancellation fences publication immediately and cooperatively stops reads; an in-flight network read can occupy its slot until the HTTP deadline. Cancelling, changing traffic selection or hiding the layer cannot publish an old frame. Turning away from the Globe cancels active camera work.
- Catalog: 4 MiB and 2,000 rows maximum, atomically refused beyond either limit. One district is held at a time, with original response bytes, SHA-256 and UTC retrieval time. Explicit counts distinguish accepted/plottable, out-of-service and invalid/unsupported rows; invalid coordinates and duplicate IDs are omitted, never replaced with plausible locations. California highway bounds reject zero-coordinate placeholders. Empty accepted results are not evidence of camera absence.
- Still: 2 MiB JPEG, at most 4,096 pixels per side and 8 million pixels; original bytes/hash, dimensions, source URL, catalog hash and retrieval time are held for one selected camera. A failed refresh retains and labels the previous successful still with its original retrieval time. Changing selection discards it. A valid header does not guarantee the browser can decode every JPEG; display failure remains explicit.
- Fifteen seconds between catalog requests and independently between still requests; no polling or retry. HTTP 401/403/404/410 disables that exact district or camera resource for the rest of the session, including a cancelled request's access response. A restricted district disables its old catalog's camera requests too. Other errors remain explicit and leave the last successful catalog intact. All failed or missing acquisition is a coverage gap.
- Original bytes stay in process memory only; no recording, disk persistence, frame export or redistribution. The existing `oia-snapshot` scheme serves only the selected visible frame at `traffic/<sha256>` with no-store headers. It reads no path and changes no source bytes. The page receives no additional remote origin, CSP permission, embedded provider page or streaming player.

Source documentation and terms checked 2026-11-05 UTC: [Caltrans CWWP2 CCTV](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/documentation/cctv/cctv.htm) explicitly makes the location/status files available for application integration at no charge and states that Caltrans traffic video/stills are neither retained nor archived. [Conditions of Use](https://dot.ca.gov/conditions-of-use) generally place Caltrans-created information in the public domain, with exceptions for third-party copyrighted material and no availability/accuracy warranty. Attribution stays visible; endorsement is not implied. Access and bounded display processing are supported; broader automated analysis, recording/retention and redistribution are separate uses not implemented or granted by this adapter.

```text
scripts/run.sh fetch      # once: locked crates + the pinned CesiumJS build (verified SHA-512)
cargo run -p oia-app --locked --offline
```

Then press Ctrl+G or choose Globe in the rail. The vendor directory is embedded into the binary at build time. `crates/oia-app/build.rs` watches `ui/`, so a fetch after an earlier build makes the next build recompile `oia-app` and embed it; `tauri::generate_context!` alone re-embeds only when the crate recompiles. The build script records the version in `ui/vendor/cesium/VERSION` (written last by the fetch scripts) as `OIA_CESIUM_BUNDLED_VERSION` and emits a Cargo warning when it is absent; `scripts/run.{sh,ps1} run` also says so before launching.

Rust reports the embedded build in `GlobeView.renderer` (`bundled_version`, and `bundle`: `present`, `absent` or `version_mismatch` against the pinned version), and the inspector's renderer panel shows it. The page injects `Cesium.js` only when the bundle is `present`. Otherwise it shows "CesiumJS build not present" or "CesiumJS build is a different version" with the command to run. This check is required because Tauri answers a missing asset with the page's own `index.html` (HTTP 200, `text/html`), so `script.onerror` never fires. Without the check the instrument showed `SyntaxError: Unexpected token '<' (Cesium.js:1)` and blamed WebGL. A script that loads but defines no `Cesium` global is reported as "CesiumJS build did not load", separately from a viewer that fails to start. Nothing else in the workbench depends on the renderer.

## What it does

- **Case.** `fixtures/globe/synthetic-valley-case.json` ([fixture README](../../fixtures/globe/README.md)) is compiled in and validated at startup by `crates/oia-app/src/globe/mod.rs`. Only `origin_class: "synthetic"` is accepted by this build. An invalid fixture is reported in the instrument as a fixture error and nothing is drawn.
- **Geometry (v2 §4).** A `point` is a 7 px dot with its uncertainty radius drawn as a ring; an `approximate_centroid` is a hollow amber dot with a dashed radius ring and a `≈ place` tag; a `region` is a striped fill with a dashed outline and no centre, and its tag leads to its northernmost vertex. Each plotted record has a callout tag (order number, label) with a one-elbow leader ending in a dot on the geometry. Records without geometry go to the **Not plotted** tray with their recorded reason and no leader. Tags whose anchors coincide on screen are stacked in display order.
- **Selection.** Click a tag, a record in the inspector, a tray entry, or the drawn geometry; Escape clears. One bracket is drawn around the projected bounds of the selected geometry, and a target card lists the record's values (`±` and `≈` in amber, "not recorded" where the fixture says so). If the selected geometry is off screen the camera flies to it.
- **Imagery.** The inspector's Imagery tab lists the registered sources from `crates/oia-app/src/globe/imagery.rs`: NASA GIBS Blue Marble (composite, no date) and VIIRS true colour (daily, UTC date chosen by the user, validated against the coverage periods GIBS published when its capabilities were read on 2026-11-03). No overlay is registered (see Validation for the withdrawn coastline layer). The attribution strip names the provider, layer, date and the tile counts this renderer observed (loaded, failed, pending, and requests Cesium cancelled because the tile left the view, which are navigation, not gaps). A day inside a published gap or in the future is refused with the reason; a day after the recorded coverage is allowed with a note that tiles may not exist yet.
- **Detail when zoomed in (resolution ladder).** Above the selected basemap, Rust sends a ladder of real, registered detail sources, coarse to fine, each with the Cesium globe level it starts at:

  | Tier | Source | Source pixels | Coverage | Drawn from | Capture |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Basemap | NASA GIBS Blue Marble or VIIRS day | 500 m / 250 m | global | every zoom | 2004 composite / the selected UTC day |
  | Detail 1 | EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2025 (`tiles.maps.eox.at`) | 10 m, requested to level 14 | global | globe level 7 (Blue Marble) or 8 (VIIRS), about 80 km altitude and closer | cloud-free yearly mosaic of 2025 Sentinel-2 scenes; per-pixel day not identified |
  | Detail 2 | USGS The National Map orthoimagery (`basemap.nationalmap.gov`) | about 1 m (NAIP), cached to level 16 | conterminous US rectangle | globe level 13, a few km altitude and closer | aerial photography flown 2017 to 2021, year varies by state |
  | Detail 3 | USGS dynamic NAIP (`imagery.nationalmap.gov`) | primarily 0.6 m, requested to level 18 | continental US bounds, gaps possible | globe level 16 | aerial mosaic; local flight date not supplied per tile |

  Each tier is drawn only where the surface tiles are at or beyond its level (`ImageryLayer.minimumTerrainLevel`) and only inside its coverage rectangle. Past its deepest published level the renderer magnifies the last tiles; it never requests the upsampled levels a provider may serve. Where a detail tile is not published (HTTP 404, for example sea or Mexico inside the US rectangle), it is counted as **not published**, separately from failures, and ancestor tiles coarser than the tier's start are refused without a request, so the layer beneath shows instead of a coarse tile magnified under the detail tier's name. The **Sharper imagery when zoomed in** checkbox (on by default) turns the ladder off, which keeps a selected VIIRS day unmixed at every zoom and stops all requests to the three detail origins.
- **Which source is on screen.** Every layer credits its source per tile, so the credits Cesium adds in a frame name the sources it actually drew. The page reads them through `CreditDisplay`'s public `beginFrame`, `addCreditToNextFrame` and `endFrame`. The attribution strip names the finest tier drawn as **on top** (cached tiers are opaque; the dynamic NAIP tier can contain transparent no-data), and coarser tiers drawn in the same frame as **beneath or at edges** (under it, at the horizon, in gaps, or while its tiles load), with each drawn source's attribution. The view-state strip's `DETAIL` cell shows the tier on top, `none drawn at this zoom`, or `off`. When a VIIRS day is selected and a detail tier is drawn, the strip adds **zoomed detail is not** *that day* in amber, and each tier's inspector entry states the time relation. The inspector's Imagery tab lists each tier with source, origin, layer, start level, nominal source resolution, coverage, capture statement, licence, attribution, terms, checked date, and its own tile counts (loaded, not published, failed, cancelled, deepest level loaded).
- **Camera.** Drag to orbit, wheel to zoom; with the globe focused, arrows pan, `+`/`-` zoom, Home returns to the case anchor, `0` shows the whole globe. After each move the page reports the camera to Rust, which validates it (finite, in range) and returns the view; the view-state strip prints the altitude from that record.
- **Effects.** The shell's Effects toggle also removes the atmosphere and fog; Clean evidence (Ctrl+Shift+E, or the canvas tool) hides tags, leaders, brackets and drawn geometry. Neither changes a value. Sun, moon, sky box and globe lighting are off so nothing on the globe reads as a live time of day for a historical case.
- **Layers.** `crates/oia-app/src/globe/layers.rs` is a Rust-owned registry of context layers. A floating Layers panel (canvas tool to show or hide it) and the inspector's Layers tab list each entry with its source, origin class, requirement, features shown of features in extent, coverage, extent, attribution, licence and statement; turning a layer on flies the camera to its recorded extent once. The case layer is always drawn. The first context layer is the **`CAM-06` mapped ALPR camera directory** from the merged `oia-alpr-map` kernel (#28) over the bundled synthetic New Meridian fixture (`fixtures/cameras/alpr/new-meridian-corridor-fixture.json`, parsed in `oia-app` and accepted only when it declares itself synthetic): the fixture's nodes go through `normalize_alpr_node`, `evaluate_viewport_query` over the fixture viewport (coverage status, manufacturer breakdown, the kernel's local filters, saturation at the 1,500-record limit), `validate_alpr_records`, and `direction_wedge_polygon`. Each camera is a small neutral square with a `CAM` tag (display id and vendor class); a translucent three-point **wedge** is drawn only from the kernel's illustrative bearing geometry (90 m, ±20°) where a numeric bearing is tagged, and never otherwise, so a missing bearing never reads as north. Selecting a camera brackets its point or wedge and the target card lists position, tagged bearing, wedge basis, vendor class, operator, zone, reference, the mapper's check date separately from the OSM edit timestamp, and the source with the synthetic marker. The inspector's filter radios (all, Flock Safety, other named, unspecified, bearing recorded) and the breakdown meters (`n / total` per class) come from the kernel; a camera the filter omits, or a layer turned off, clears its selection. The layer statement says that mapped nodes are assertions, not observed hardware, feeds or plate reads, and that absence from the layer is not absence of cameras.

## Boundary

Rust owns the state (ADR 0008 §4 rule 1): the parsed case, the imagery registry and selection, the layer registry with each layer's visibility and the ALPR local filter, the camera and the selected record or camera feature live in `GlobeState`, exposed through nine commands (`globe_view`, `globe_select`, `globe_camera`, `globe_imagery`, `globe_layer`, `globe_alpr_filter`, and `globe_annotate`, `globe_annotation_revise`, `globe_annotation_delete` for [analyst annotations](#analyst-annotations-165)) that return the whole `GlobeView` in the `oia-local-tools/1` envelope. `ui/globe.js` draws that view and sends intents; its own state is the Cesium objects, projected screen positions, the overlays and layers-panel toggles and tile counters it observed. It computes no domain value: positions, radii, wedge vertices, times, zones and labels are printed as sent, and the only arithmetic is projection to the screen and camera motion.

Geometry and time types are provisional pending `#12` (`oia-time-geo`), kept to a validated WGS84 point, a reported-time record with a precision kind, and a camera view. They are not a second domain model and should be replaced by the shared primitives when they merge.

| Bound | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Records per case | 512 |
| Region ring | 3 to 256 vertices |
| ALPR fixture elements | 2,000; the kernel's 1,500-record viewport limit reports saturation beyond it |
| Text fields | 2,048 bytes |
| Camera height | 50 m to 60,000 km |
| Remote origins | three, each registered in `imagery.rs` and allow-listed in the CSP: `https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov`, `https://tiles.maps.eox.at`, `https://basemap.nationalmap.gov`; a unit test fails if the CSP's remote origins differ from the registry |
| Tile requests | at most 6 in flight per origin (`RequestScheduler.requestsByServer`, from the registry's `max_requests_in_flight`, down from Cesium's default 18; GIBS asks for no bulk use, and the detail origins publish no rate figure) and only for the visible view; no prefetch beyond it |
| Detail levels requested | Sentinel-2 cloudless: from the tier's start to 14; USGS orthoimagery: 13 to 16, inside the conterminous US rectangle only |
| Renderer messages kept | 24, shown in the inspector |

## Renderer

CesiumJS 1.147.0 (Apache-2.0, [notices §3](../../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)) is downloaded by `scripts/fetch-ui-vendor.{sh,ps1}` into the gitignored `crates/oia-app/ui/vendor/cesium/`, after the npm tarball's SHA-512 is verified. Only the minified browser build, `Assets/`, `Workers/` and `ThirdParty/` are kept with the licence files. Cesium Ion is disabled (no token, no Ion assets); terrain is the WGS84 ellipsoid; the widget stylesheet is replaced by six rules in `ui/globe.css`.

Two things were needed to run the bundle under the workbench CSP (`default-src 'self'`, no `'unsafe-inline'`, no `'unsafe-eval'`), both recorded here so that a Cesium upgrade re-examines them:

1. **Fetch-time patch.** The bundled Knockout library resolves the global object with `var t=this||(0,eval)("this")` while the bundle loads; `eval` is refused and the whole bundle fails to define `Cesium`. The fetch scripts replace that expression with `var t=globalThis`, require that it matches exactly once, and fail otherwise. OIA uses no Cesium widget, so no Knockout binding (which would also use `new Function`) is exercised.
2. **Worker path.** The combined build also ships its worker code as a string (`globalThis.CESIUM_WORKERS`) and, when it is present, starts workers from `blob:` URLs with `importScripts`, which `script-src 'self'` refuses. `ui/globe.js` deletes that global after the bundle loads, so Cesium starts the same-origin module workers from `vendor/cesium/Workers/` instead.

The CSP gained `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for Cesium's bundled WebAssembly modules (compiled from files, not strings), `blob:` in `img-src` (Cesium turns fetched tile bytes into object URLs when it cannot use `createImageBitmap`), `'self'` in `connect-src` for the renderer's own asset fetches (`Assets/*.json`), and the registered imagery origin in `connect-src` and `img-src`. Inline style attributes that Cesium's unused widget code tries to set remain blocked and are listed in the inspector's renderer messages; they have no effect on the instrument. `worker-src` stays `'self'`: with `CESIUM_WORKERS` removed, every worker Cesium starts for this instrument is a same-origin module worker, and the only blob-worker code left in the bundle is the meshopt decoder pool, which is used for 3D Tiles and never reached here.

## Honesty rules applied

- Nothing drawn from the fixture is an observation; the case chip says `synthetic` and every inspector view names the fixture path.
- A record without recorded geometry never receives a position (tray only). A region never receives a centre. An approximate centroid is labelled `≈ place` with its radius and basis.
- Imagery is display context: the capture statement says what the pixels are (a 2004 composite; a daily VIIRS mosaic for a UTC day with variable overpass time; a 2025 Sentinel-2 yearly mosaic; 2017 to 2021 aerial orthoimagery) and that none of it shows the case period. A missing tile or an unpublished day is a coverage gap, never an empty scene.
- Sharper imagery when zoomed in is always a different registered source with finer source pixels, never the basemap resampled. Its capture period is named whenever it is drawn, and it is never labelled with the basemap's date. Nominal source resolution is the provider's statement, not a measurement of the pixels on screen.
- Tile counts are what this renderer observed in this session, labelled as such. The attribution strip carries the GIBS acknowledgement, and the licence, terms URL and capabilities check date are in the inspector.
- Camera altitude is a view setting from the Rust record, not a measurement. No terrain, labels, roads or tracks are drawn, because none is registered.

## Analyst annotations (#165)

Implemented for `CASE-03` (save annotations; source records stay unchanged) and the drawing subset of `GEO-01`, after God's Eye View's manual whiteboard tool ([prior art](../prior-art.md#map-whiteboard-annotations-srcannotations)). The analyst draws a **pin**, **line**, **area** or **range ring** on the globe, labels it, optionally adds a note and a case record it is about, and saves it.

- **Drawing.** *Annotate* on the canvas toolbar, or `A` with the globe focused, opens the draw bar in the target card's place. Click the globe to add vertices; a dashed rubber band follows the pointer. For the keyboard, pan the view under the centre crosshair with the arrow keys and press `V`. Enter or a double-click finishes a line or area, Backspace removes the last vertex, Escape cancels. A pin finishes on its vertex, a range ring on its edge. The draft is presentation state in its own Cesium data source until it is saved; the page refocuses the globe after each click because Cesium's canvas does not take focus. Starting to draw turns Clean evidence off, visibly, so the result is not hidden.
- **Rust owns them.** `crates/oia-app/src/globe/annotations.rs` keeps the session's annotations in `GlobeState` and revalidates the whole draft on save: finite WGS84 vertices through `oia_time_geo::GeoPointWgs84`; 2 to 512 line vertices and 3 to 256 area vertices; consecutive vertices at least 0.5 m apart, which also refuses an area whose last vertex repeats its first; no segment longer than 19,000 km (ends nearly antipodal, so the great-circle path is ambiguous); a ring radius from 0.5 m to 10,000 km; a one-line label of up to 256 bytes and a note of up to 2,048; a referenced record that exists; at most 120 annotations. A refused draft changes nothing and stays on screen with the reason. Nothing is clamped, snapped or simplified.
- **Measures.** Rust computes a line's length, an area's perimeter and a ring's radius as great-circle distances on the IUGG mean sphere (`oia-time-geo`, R = 6,371,008.8 m) and sends a ring's outline as spherical destination points every 5° of bearing, so the page computes no geometry. The card and inspector label each measure *spherical*; it is not an ellipsoidal geodesic, a survey or a terrain distance. Area (square metres) and self-crossing rings are not computed or checked in this slice. Cesium draws lines and outlines as ellipsoid geodesics between the stored vertices, which can differ slightly from the spherical measure.
- **Not evidence.** Each annotation is `ObjectType::Annotation` (`oia_domain::evidence`), tagged `◆ A<n>` with a dashed tag and neutral marks, and its card says *analyst annotation*, *not an observation* and *session only*. There is no colour choice: amber, red and violet keep their v2 §5.1 meanings and the accent stays the selection colour. Referencing a case record does not change it; a targeted test compares every plotted record before and after.
- **Layer, selection and editing.** *Analyst annotations* is a layer in the Layers panel and inspector with its count; hiding it, or Clean evidence, hides every mark and tag, and hiding it clears an annotation selection. Selecting a tag or a list entry shows the card (shape, vertices, measure, record, OIA creation time in UTC) and the inspector's **Notes** tab, where the label, note and record reference can be revised (the revision number and time are kept) and an annotation, or all of them, deleted. Geometry is not edited in place; redraw it. Ids are `ann-<n>` and are not reused after a delete.
- **Session only.** Annotations live in memory and are lost when OIA closes, as the layer, card and Notes tab say. Durable storage waits on `#11`/`#14`, and export into report bundles on `#22`/`#39`. The creation time is OIA's clock (Unix milliseconds, UTC); a clock reading before 1970 is refused rather than recorded.

| --- | --- |

| Line / area vertices | 2 to 512 / 3 to 256 |
| Consecutive vertex spacing | at least 0.5 m |
| Segment length | at most 19,000 km |
| Ring radius | 0.5 m to 10,000 km |
| Label / note | 256 / 2,048 bytes |

## Closer detail and source products (2026-11-03, #23)

Implemented for `GEO-02` and the `SPACE-02` presentation subset: the detail ladder now adds the [USGS NAIP dynamic image service](https://imagery.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/USGSNAIPImagery/ImageServer), above the older level-16 tile cache. Visible-view exports use Web Mercator tile bounds, 256 × 256 PNG32, natural-colour bands 0/1/2 and nearest-neighbour sampling. The tier begins at globe level 16 and caps imagery requests at 18; closer views magnify existing pixels. The service describes primarily 0.6 m aerial orthophotography, with variable local resolution and flight date. Its 0.3 m metadata grid is not evidence of 0.3 m native detail. This improves continental-US detail; the global satellite tier remains Sentinel-2 at 10 m. Other regions and sub-metre satellite providers remain future source work under D06.

The new origin is registered, attributed, bounded to the continental-US rectangle and limited to six concurrent requests. Transparent no-data exposes the layer below, not an empty observed scene; credits indicate a rendered layer, not opaque coverage of every pixel. Capture statements explicitly decline a case-period or selected-day match. Existing HTTP failure/cancellation counters apply. Inherited N1 elapsed tile deadlines remain open; concurrency alone does not bound elapsed requests.

The Imagery inspector defaults to **Neutral imagery display**, disabling ground atmosphere, sky atmosphere and fog. Turning it off restores those display effects unless reduced effects are enabled. Layer brightness/contrast/saturation/gamma are neutral and hue is zero. Lighting remains disabled. Provider mosaicking, colour mapping, orthorectification, JPEG compression and display projection are not undone. The separate Clean evidence control hides case overlays; neither control edits source data or measurements.
