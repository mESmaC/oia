OPEN INTELLIGENCE AGENCY TECHNICAL REPORT
REPORT ID: TR-2026-0881
CLASSIFICATION: UNCLASSIFIED // PUBLIC RELEASE
DATE: 2026-09-14
SUBJECT: OBSERVATIONAL ANALYSIS OF UNUSUAL X-BAND RADAR REFLECTIONS IN THE GULF OF MAINE

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On 2026-08-12 between 03:14 UTC and 04:22 UTC, coastal meteorological radar stations NEXRAD KCBW and KGYX registered anomalous non-precipitating echoes exhibiting radial velocities exceeding 450 meters per second. Simultaneous thermal infrared observations from NOAA-21 revealed localized sea-surface temperature anomalies of +1.8 Kelvin along the track of the radar returns.

2. INSTRUMENTATION AND METHODOLOGY
Data were collected using dual-polarization Doppler radar operating in volume coverage pattern 212. Target cross-sections were normalized against sea-clutter reference baselines established over the preceding 30-day baseline period. Atmospheric soundings from Portland Jetport (PWM) confirmed a sharp thermal inversion layer between 850 hPa and 700 hPa with a temperature step of 6.2 degrees Celsius.

3. MEASURED FINDINGS
- At 03:22:15 UTC, target echo Charlie-1 exhibited an apparent velocity of 482 m/s (+/- 15 m/s) at an altitude of 3,200 meters above mean sea level.
- At 03:41:00 UTC, the radar cross-section spiked to 42.5 dBZ with a differential reflectivity (ZDR) of -1.4 dB, inconsistent with standard biological or hydrometeor scatterers.
- Total observed transit duration across the coastal surveillance corridor was 68 minutes before signal dissolution at coordinates 43.12N, 69.85W.

4. DECLARED LIMITATIONS
Radar refraction effects caused by the documented ducting inversion layer could not be completely decoupled from kinetic target motion. Secondary multipath reflections from sea-surface chop introduce an estimated positional uncertainty of up to 1,200 meters in range. Ground-truth optical corroboration was precluded by overcast cloud ceilings below 1,000 meters.

5. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS
1. Did the thermal sea-surface anomaly precede or follow the passage of the primary radar return?
2. What physical mechanism accounts for the negative differential reflectivity (-1.4 dB) in the absence of frozen hydrometeors?
3. Were maritime Automatic Identification System (AIS) vessel transponders subjected to localized radio-frequency interference during the event interval?
